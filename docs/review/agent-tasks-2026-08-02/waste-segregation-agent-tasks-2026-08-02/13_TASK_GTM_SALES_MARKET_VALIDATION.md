# Task: GTM, Saleability and Market Validation

## Priority

P1 in parallel with product definition. Founder-led work is required.

## Objective

Validate that a specific buyer will pay for segregation assurance and evidence before building a broad compliance suite.

## Market thesis

### External trigger

Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 became effective on 1 April 2026 and introduced four-stream source segregation plus stronger Bulk Waste Generator accountability and digital tracking concepts.

### Competitive reality

The market already contains:

- waste processors with collection/compliance platforms;
- BWG compliance dashboards;
- EPR platforms;
- consumer recycling assistants;
- reward/habit products;
- smart-bin hardware.

Examples found during the review include ORI by MSGP, CivicLoop, Recykal, CleanIt, Bower and Recycle Coach.

Therefore, “AI waste scanner” and “BWG dashboard” are not sufficient differentiation.

### Proposed wedge

**Last-metre segregation assurance for sites and processors.**

Differentiate on:

- item-level uncertainty resolution;
- four-stream education;
- contamination evidence;
- operator action workflow;
- reviewed corrections;
- multilingual training;
- measurable improvement;
- integration into an existing processor/compliance workflow.

## Initial customer hypotheses

Rank and test:

1. authorised waste processors serving Bengaluru BWGs;
2. apartment complexes with active management and recurring compliance pain;
3. facility-management firms;
4. schools/campuses;
5. hotels/restaurants.

A processor/partner may be a better first buyer because it aggregates sites and already owns collection/compliance relationships.

## Interview plan

Minimum 20 conversations:

- 5 processors/vendors;
- 5 apartment decision-makers;
- 5 operators/housekeeping supervisors;
- 5 other BWGs.

Do not pitch first. Ask for the current workflow.

### Questions

- What changed after SWM Rules 2026?
- What evidence must you currently maintain?
- Where does segregation fail?
- Who checks bins and how?
- What happens after contamination is found?
- Which records are manual?
- Which report is painful?
- What creates fines, disputes or vendor conflict?
- Who owns the budget?
- What existing software is used?
- What would make a pilot unacceptable?
- What result would justify payment?

### Evidence standard

Record:

- exact workflow;
- frequency;
- current cost/time;
- named decision-maker;
- current alternative;
- severity;
- willingness to pilot;
- willingness to pay;
- procurement constraint;
- quote or observation;
- confidence level.

## Offer tests

### Offer A: segregation audit pilot

- baseline site audit;
- four-stream training;
- app-assisted checks;
- weekly evidence report;
- improvement review.

### Offer B: processor engagement layer

White-labelled scanning, training and issue management for the processor's client sites.

### Offer C: school/CSR programme

Sponsored education and verified behaviour programme. Treat as a channel/engagement model, not the primary product until the buyer is clear.

## Pricing experiments

Use price discovery, not cost-plus guessing.

Test:

- one-time paid audit/pilot;
- monthly per-site licence;
- per-active-site partner licence;
- implementation/support fee;
- annual contract discount.

A Bengaluru competitor advertises roughly ₹3,999/month per complex, which is a useful reference point, not proof of the correct price for this product.

Present at least three materially different packages. Ask which budget line and approval path applies.

## Sales assets

Create:

- one-page problem brief;
- 90-second demo;
- pilot scope;
- data/privacy sheet;
- security summary;
- sample weekly report;
- ROI worksheet;
- implementation checklist;
- support/SLA draft.

Avoid feature lists. Lead with the operational outcome.

## Pilot success criteria

Before starting, agree on:

- checklist completion;
- evidence coverage;
- contamination-rate change;
- resolution time;
- training completion;
- operator adoption;
- buyer report use;
- renewal decision date.

A pilot without a conversion decision is a demo.

## Brand task

“ReLoop” has substantial collision risk across recycling apps and businesses.

Run:

1. app-store search;
2. web/domain search;
3. Indian trademark search;
4. social-handle search;
5. pronunciation and language check;
6. buyer confusion test.

Do not rename impulsively, but do not spend on branding under the current name until the screen is complete.

## Kill criteria

Pause or change wedge if:

- fewer than 3 of 10 qualified buyers describe the problem as urgent;
- no buyer owns a budget;
- existing processor software already solves the full workflow;
- evidence/reporting is not used in any decision;
- the buyer wants collection operations rather than software;
- paid pilot conversion remains absent after well-qualified outreach.

## Acceptance criteria

- Interview evidence is stored in a structured repository.
- One ICP and buyer are selected.
- One painful repeated job is documented.
- A paid-pilot offer is sent to qualified prospects.
- Pricing is based on buyer feedback.
- Competitive differentiation is explicit.
- Brand collision has a documented decision.
- Product backlog is changed based on evidence, not defended from it.

## Founder-only actions

Agents may prepare research, scripts, trackers and collateral. The founder must conduct or directly supervise buyer interviews, pricing conversations and pilot commitments.
