# Task: GTM and Saleability Validation

## Priority

P1, founder-led in parallel with product contract.

## Objective

Find a buyer with a current, budgeted segregation-quality problem and convert a measurable pilot.

## Current market position

The market already has authorised processors/platforms offering:

- registration;
- collection;
- GPS/vehicle tracking;
- certificates;
- invoices;
- payments;
- complaints;
- compliance records.

Competing directly requires operational authorisation, logistics and municipal integration.

The app should be the quality/engagement layer integrated before handover.

## Discovery segments

### Authorised processors

Questions:

- what percentage of incoming waste is contaminated?
- how is evidence captured?
- which client sites create repeated problems?
- what does poor segregation cost?
- do processors provide training?
- what data would reduce rejection/rework?
- can a white-label client app improve renewal?

### Apartments/facilities

Questions:

- who measures waste for BWG declaration?
- where does four-stream segregation fail?
- what creates notices/charges/vendor conflict?
- how are staff trained?
- what records are maintained?
- what cannot current society software do?
- who approves a pilot?

### Society software platforms

Question:

- would waste quality be a module/API rather than a standalone app?

## Minimum evidence set

Conduct at least:

- 5 processor/operator interviews;
- 5 apartment/facility buyers;
- 5 on-ground housekeeping/waste-room observations;
- 5 adjacent partners/consultants/software platforms.

Record:

- exact workflow;
- frequency;
- current alternative;
- cost/time;
- severity;
- authority/budget;
- procurement;
- data/privacy objection;
- willingness to pilot;
- willingness to pay;
- decision date.

## Offer tests

### Offer A: segregation baseline and improvement pilot

- site baseline;
- operator checks;
- uncertain-item assistant;
- staff training;
- weekly evidence;
- end-state comparison.

### Offer B: processor client-quality module

- white-label;
- client sites;
- incoming contamination evidence;
- training;
- processor dashboard/API.

### Offer C: society-software add-on

- embedded scan;
- issue workflow;
- waste scorecard;
- report/export.

## Pricing discovery

Test materially different structures:

- fixed paid pilot;
- monthly/site;
- monthly/household with minimum;
- processor portfolio licence;
- implementation/support;
- outcome-linked component only where measurable and contractually safe.

Do not anchor solely on consumer ₹99/₹299 plans.

## Sales assets

- one-page pain/outcome brief;
- 90-second workflow demo;
- sample site report;
- security/privacy sheet;
- policy-source statement;
- pilot scope;
- measurement plan;
- implementation checklist;
- integration contract;
- explicit limitation: not an authorised processor or official certificate unless applicable.

## Kill criteria

Change direction if:

- processors say contamination is not material;
- buyers do not maintain or use evidence;
- no budget owner exists;
- existing processor platform already solves pre-handover quality;
- pilot requires collection operations you do not own;
- no qualified paid commitment after repeated targeted offers.

## Acceptance criteria

- one ICP selected;
- one repeated costly job;
- buyer authority and budget identified;
- paid proposal sent;
- success and renewal trigger agreed;
- backlog changed based on evidence;
- founder quotes/redirects recorded verbatim in durable docs.

## Anything else?

Bengaluru's current BWG declaration, vendor and waste-charge confusion is a discovery opening, not proof that software alone solves it. Validate the operational cause before building.
